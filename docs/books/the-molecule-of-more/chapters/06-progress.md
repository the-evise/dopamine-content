# The Molecule of More — Chapter 6: Progress

## The central idea

**The drive that helps us build a better life can make it hard to enjoy the life we’ve built.** The authors use dopamine to connect exploration, invention, and ambition. Each begins with the ability to imagine a possibility beyond what’s already here. Once an improvement becomes familiar, though, attention can move to the next one before we’ve had much time to enjoy it.

The chapter starts with early human migration. The authors discuss research linking a variant of the dopamine receptor gene *DRD4* with how far some ancestral populations migrated. They favor the hypothesis that the variant may have helped people adapt along the way. The association leaves the causes of migration open. Later, the chapter looks at entrepreneurship and asks what happens when technology makes new experiences and goods available faster than we can appreciate them.

## The stories

### Tocqueville and the house sold before the roof went on

In nineteenth-century America, Alexis de Tocqueville noticed a restlessness that persisted even among people who were doing well. He pictured a man building a house for his later years, then selling it before the roof was finished. The same man plants a garden, then rents it out as the trees begin to bear fruit.

The house and the garden were supposed to give him something to enjoy. By the time either was ready, his attention had already moved to the next thing. The authors use the portrait to show how easily the pursuit itself can become the part someone keeps returning for.

### Isabel Allende on starting over

The chapter quotes writer Isabel Allende on what emigration felt like. The supports she’d relied on fell away, and her past carried little weight with the people she met. She had to begin again. Her account adds a human dimension to the chapter’s discussion of exploration. The opportunity to start somewhere new can come with the work of rebuilding a familiar life.

## Dopamine, habits, and reset

The chapter offers no reset, detox, or fasting protocol. Its closest connection is **the habit of looking for a better option while you’re still in the middle of the one you chose**. You put on a song, then start browsing for the next one before the chorus. The search gives you a fresh possibility every few seconds, while the song needs time to unfold.

One way to explore that habit is to choose a song in advance and let it finish before opening the queue. Notice when you want to switch, what you expect the next song to give you, and whether staying changes how much you enjoy the one you picked.

## Enjoyment and its opposite

The chapter imagines someone checking a weather app while overlooking the sky and the feel of the morning air. The forecast answers a real question about what’s coming. Looking up gives you a different experience of the same morning. The authors call that kind of sensory pleasure *here-and-now* enjoyment.

For **Dopamine Personality**, this adds a question to **Novelty ↔ Familiarity**. Someone can love the novelty of choosing a new recipe and also get a lot of pleasure from making a familiar one. It’s worth asking about both the anticipation of trying something and the experience of doing it.

**Reflection prompt.** Think of something you were excited to get or finish. When it finally became part of your daily life, what did you enjoy about it? How soon did you start thinking about the next improvement?
