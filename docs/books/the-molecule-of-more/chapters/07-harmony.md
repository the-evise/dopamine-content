# The Molecule of More — Chapter 7: Harmony

## The central idea

Chapter 6 asked why we keep looking for the next improvement. In the final chapter, the authors ask **what lets us enjoy getting better at something and enjoy doing it today**.

In their framework, dopamine helps us imagine what we could make, learn, or change. What they call *here-and-now* processes let us take in what’s happening through our senses, emotions, and relationships. These can reinforce each other. A pianist who wants to play a passage more expressively has to listen closely to how it sounds now. That attention is part of the pleasure of playing, and it gives the pianist something specific to work on.

This is why **mastery** matters to the authors. A skill can keep giving you new challenges while also letting you feel the satisfaction of something you can do well today.

## The stories

### The finance executive who started painting again

A financial-services executive spent his working days thinking about options, exchange rates, and other abstractions. He was wealthy and miserable. After seeking help, he returned to painting, a hobby he’d abandoned decades earlier. Soon he was looking forward to getting home. One evening he painted for four hours and barely noticed the time pass.

Painting still gave him a problem to solve. It also gave him something to see as he worked. He could put down a color, see what it did to the rest of the picture, and decide what the painting needed next.

### Jake Sully finds something worth using his skills for

In the authors’ reading of *Avatar*, Jake starts out working for a mining company that sees Pandora as a source of resources. As he grows close to the Na’vi, he begins to understand what the company’s plans would cost them. When the Tree of Souls is threatened, he uses the planning and coordination skills he brought with him to help protect the people and place he’s come to care about.

## Dopamine, habits, and reset

The chapter offers no reset, detox, or fasting protocol. Its practical focus is **where your attention goes during an activity and during a break**.

In one experiment the chapter describes, students paused a concentration task for 40 seconds. Those who viewed a picture of a planted rooftop made fewer errors afterward than those who viewed a concrete rooftop. The study measured task performance. For a break from scrolling, the related question is what you do with the pause. If you usually check your phone between tasks, try looking out the window for one break and notice how it feels to return to what you were doing.

## Enjoyment and its opposite

The chapter describes two ways to miss out on enjoyment. You can be so focused on future results that the work feels like something to get through. Or you can settle into easy entertainment that feels pleasant while it lasts but gives you little reason to look forward to doing it again. The painting story shows another possibility. **An activity can hold your attention now and give you a reason to come back tomorrow.**

For **Dopamine Personality**, that adds a question to **Recognition ↔ Personal satisfaction**. Think of rewriting a quiz result until it captures an experience you’ve struggled to put into words. There’s satisfaction in reading the line and recognizing that it finally says what you meant. Seeing a reader respond to it later can add another kind of enjoyment.

**Reflection prompt.** What do you enjoy while you’re doing it, even on a day when you make little progress? What keeps you curious enough to return to it?
