# The Molecule of More — Chapter 3: Domination

## The central idea

Chapter 2 asks why a reward can feel so urgent. Chapter 3 asks **what lets us choose among competing rewards and keep working toward one that’s still far away**.

The authors call the first process **desire dopamine** and the second **control dopamine**. In their framework, desire makes a possibility compelling. Control lets you imagine different outcomes, make a plan, and adjust it when things go wrong. They use *domination* broadly to mean shaping a situation to reach a goal. That can involve persistence, cooperation, or making a tempting choice harder to reach in the first place.

## The stories and experiments

### The rats that liked the treat but worked less for it

When a treat cost one lever press, rats with reduced dopamine activity readily ate it. As researchers raised the number of presses required, those rats worked less for the treat than the comparison group. Given a choice, they were more likely to eat bland food that was already available. The authors use the experiment to distinguish **how rewarding something is when you get it** from **how much effort you’ll spend to get it**.

### James — Cooperation becomes something he values

James is a driven executive whose anger is putting his job at risk. With his therapist’s help, he starts greeting coworkers and being more considerate when the team works late. At first, he does it because it makes working together easier. Then he finds he likes the warmth he gets back. One day, a colleague comes to him for comfort after discovering someone has opened a credit account in her name. The relationship has begun to offer something he hadn’t planned for.

### The manager who wanted another mess to fix

A manager spends years turning around a chaotic department. Once it runs smoothly, she tries to enjoy the calmer environment she created. Within months, she asks to transfer to another department in trouble. Fixing the problem was absorbing. Living with the result gave her much less of what she enjoyed.

## The dopamine concepts

| Concept | What the chapter adds |
| --- | --- |
| **Self-efficacy** | A small early success can change whether you believe your effort will pay off. That belief can make it easier to persist. |
| **Planning ahead of an urge** | In the chapter’s recovery examples, people identify cues and make arrangements in advance, such as bringing someone supportive to a difficult event. That changes what they have to decide once the urge arrives. |
| **Working together and enjoying company** | The authors distinguish the reward of achieving something together from the pleasure of spending time together. James’s story shows how both can develop in the same relationship. |

## What this adds to Dopamine Personality

**The work of making progress can be its own reward.** Someone can stay with a single project for months because they enjoy figuring out each difficult part, then lose interest once it runs smoothly. That’s useful for your **Depth ↔ Variety** axis. It helps separate a preference for switching activities from a preference for staying with a problem while there’s still something to solve.

**“Think of a project you loved working on. Once it was finished, did you enjoy what you’d made, or were you already looking for another problem to solve?”**
