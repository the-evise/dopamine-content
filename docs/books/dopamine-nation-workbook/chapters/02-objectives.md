# Chapter 2 — Objectives

## Summary

A behavior can solve an immediate problem. It may help you feel less alone, less bored, or less tense. Lembke asks you to take that reason seriously and compare the result with what you hoped would happen. A habit can deliver some relief in the moment and fall short later in the evening or the following morning.

## Reflection prompts

- What did you want to feel more or less of the last time you reached for it?
- At what point did it give you something you wanted? When did that change?
- Does the same activity serve different purposes at different times?
- What would tell you that you actually got what you needed?

## Worked example

| Occasion | What Mina wanted | What she got right away | What happened later | Gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday after a late work call | A short break from thinking about work | A funny clip made her laugh | She kept browsing for 45 minutes and was still alert at bedtime | She got a break and had trouble winding down |
| Saturday in bed | Something to do when she felt lonely | Familiar voices and a sense of company | After 80 minutes she still wanted to talk to someone | She got company for the moment and still wanted a conversation |

Mina realizes she had described both nights as relaxation. The first was about switching off from work. The second was about feeling less alone.
