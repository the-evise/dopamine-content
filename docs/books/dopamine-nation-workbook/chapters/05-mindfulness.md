# Chapter 5 — Mindfulness

## Summary

Lembke uses mindfulness to observe urges, sensations, and thoughts as they happen. During a pause, the reasons for making an exception can sound convincing. The chapter’s exercise asks you to record those reasons and answer them with what you know about the habit and why you chose to pause.

## Reflection prompts

- What sentence tends to appear just before you make an exception?
- Where do you notice the urge in your body? What changes if you wait and observe it for a little while?
- What part of the thought is true? What part leaves out something you know from past experience?
- What response would you find believable while the urge is still there?

## Worked example

After a hard call, Mina thinks one video will get work out of her head. She notices she’s tense and reaching for the phone. She writes a reply she can believe. A video might make her laugh. On similar nights, one often became 45 minutes. She writes down the thought about work that keeps returning, then opens her book. The urge is still there for a while. By the time she stops reading, she’s less preoccupied with it.
