# Chapter 4 — Abstinence and Asceticism

## Summary

This chapter plans the pause. Lembke distinguishes the target habit from the cues that lead into it and from the activities you want to make more room for. **Self-binding** means setting a boundary in advance, such as putting something out of reach or choosing an alternative before an urge arrives. The chapter also looks at how a manageable challenge can feel different before, during, and afterward. Lembke calls the broader idea of adapting to a moderate challenge *hormesis* and discusses the risk of pushing a challenge too far.

## Reflection prompts

- What exactly are you pausing? Which ordinary uses of the same device or setting still serve a purpose?
- What usually happens just before the urge? Consider the place, time, feeling, and activity that came before it.
- What do you want to have more time or attention for? Which option would still feel worthwhile without a big payoff?
- Who could check in without making the experiment feel like a test you have to pass?

## Worked example

Mina chooses a four-week pause from video browsing after 10 p.m. Work calls and messages are outside that boundary. The target in her first circle is browsing videos in bed. The second includes recommendation links and checking her phone right after a stressful call. Her outer circle includes calling a friend, reading on paper, and getting enough sleep to feel alert in the morning.

She charges the phone away from the bed. If a recommendation arrives after ten, she saves it and decides the next day whether she still wants to see it. She asks a friend to check in on Sundays. On one evening she starts a paper book while still restless, then notices she feels sleepier ten minutes later. She records how it went, including the restless start.
