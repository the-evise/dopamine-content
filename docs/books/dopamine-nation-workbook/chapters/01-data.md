# Chapter 1 — Data

## Summary

Lembke starts with what happened. A week of day-by-day records is more specific than an estimate of a typical day. The chapter also looks further back. A timeline can show when the activity first mattered, what changed as it became more frequent, and what you now regret giving less time to.

## Reflection prompts

- Which activity would you investigate if you could choose only one? What counts as an instance of it?
- Which part of your estimate comes from a record, such as a screen-time log or calendar? Where are you guessing?
- When did the pattern first begin to take up more time? What else changed around then?
- What do you still feel good about afterward? What do you wish you’d done with some of that time?

## Worked example

Mina counts online video browsing after 10 p.m. She checks her screen-time log and records **45, 35, 60, 0, 55, 80, and 25 minutes** from Monday through Sunday. That’s five hours across six nights. She used to watch videos mostly on weekends. The pattern moved into weeknights when she started bringing her phone to bed after late work calls. She likes a few of the videos she remembers. What she regrets is how often she put off sleep for clips she can no longer recall.
