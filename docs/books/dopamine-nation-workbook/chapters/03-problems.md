# Chapter 3 — Problems

## Summary

Lembke uses the pleasure–pain balance to explain why the costs of a rewarding activity can appear after the experience is over. She asks readers to look for changes such as needing a longer session for the same feeling, having trouble stopping, or giving up things they care about to keep going. A specific example helps you see which cost you’ve observed and which one you’re still trying to understand.

## Reflection prompts

- When has the activity lasted longer than you intended? What did you put off that time?
- Is the activity less satisfying than it used to be? How can you tell?
- What changed in your sleep, concentration, relationships, or time for other interests?
- What else might explain the changes you’ve noticed? What would give you a fair comparison?

## Worked example

Mina remembers watching one clip and feeling ready for bed. Now she often looks for another once the first stops being funny. On the night she browsed for 80 minutes, she postponed sleep and woke tired. She also had a heavy workweek, so she can’t tell how much of her tiredness came from videos. She decides to track bedtime and morning energy during her pause, alongside any unusually late workdays.
