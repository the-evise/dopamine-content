## Learning to hide

When Jacob first sits down with Anna Lembke, he tells her he had hoped his doctor would be a man. He is embarrassed to explain why he is there. To make sense of the life he has been keeping secret, he starts with his childhood.

After his first communion, Jacob began confessing to a priest about masturbating. He promised to stop and usually managed a few days. As a teenager, he kept a drawing of Aphrodite hidden under the floorboards in the attic. At eighteen, he moved in with his older sister to study physics and engineering. She worked during the day, and he spent long stretches alone. He built a device that could prolong sexual stimulation for hours. When he decided he’d had enough, he would dismantle it or throw it away. Soon afterward, he would find the pieces and put it back together.

## What changed when access got easier

Jacob left his first machine behind when he married. He and his wife started a computer business, had two children, and moved to Germany for his work as a scientist. The internet later made pornography easy to find. One night before a conference, he stayed up watching it instead of preparing. He arrived without sleep or a finished talk and nearly lost his job. He started putting reminders around hotel rooms, hoping to interrupt the habit when he traveled.

His first wife later developed brain cancer, and Jacob cared for her until she died. He remarried and divided his time between his home in Seattle and work in Silicon Valley. When he was away, he returned to pornography and began making more elaborate devices. An online community added new possibilities and company. He enjoyed building and experimenting as well as the stimulation itself. When his wife learned what was happening, he stopped for about a month. He resumed in secret, and she eventually left. By the time he meets Lembke, the experience regularly falls short of what he imagines, and he still feels driven to repeat it.

## A hotel room after a year away

After a year without his old routine, Jacob travels to see family. He feels caught between his wife and his children in an argument about money. Alone in his hotel room, he turns on the television to watch tennis. He sees a nude image while flipping channels, and the urges return. At home he skips work, watches videos, and rebuilds the machine. In the middle of a long session, his family worries recede. Afterward, he wants to stop and finds himself retrieving the parts he’s thrown out.

This time he disposes of the machine where he can’t get it back. He also avoids media that can lead him toward the old routine and turns to his sponsor and recovery group. After his relapse, he increases his involvement with Sexaholics Anonymous, attending meetings and speaking regularly with other members. Nearly six months later, he and his wife are seeing each other again. He tells Lembke he feels able to decide what to do with his time.

## A missing shower-curtain ring

After they move back in together, Jacob’s wife asks about a missing shower-curtain ring. He knows he used it for one of his devices months earlier. He worries the answer will undo the trust they have rebuilt. He also remembers the promise he made before she returned. He explains where the ring went. She thanks him for telling her and hugs him.
