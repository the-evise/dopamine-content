## Keeping two versions of the same day

Growing up, Maria takes care of her younger siblings and tries to make life at home look ordinary. Her mother drinks heavily. Her father disappears from family life for several years, and Maria keeps the circumstances private. By the time she develops a drinking problem in her twenties, she is practiced at keeping one version of events at home and another for everyone else.

Her husband, Diego, remembers her turning on the shower to cover the sound of opening beer bottles. Maria also lies about small things that have little to do with drinking, like where she is going or what she ate for breakfast. She eventually gets sober with the help of Alcoholics Anonymous and a sponsor. By the time she sees Lembke, she has already begun building a different way of handling the truth.

## Her brother’s package

Maria comes home from work to find a package for her younger brother, Mario. She lives with him and Diego, and she knows Mario dislikes it when she opens his mail. She tells herself she deserves a little pleasure after work and opens it anyway. Then she reseals it. When Mario asks, she denies it twice, and he goes to his room angry.

Maria sleeps badly. The next morning, she finds both men in the kitchen and tells Mario she knew whose package it was, opened it anyway, and lied when he asked. She asks his forgiveness. Later, she can’t remember what was in the box. She remembers the relief of telling him and sees it as a step toward a closer relationship.

## Seeing what had changed

Diego tells Lembke that seeing Maria get sober helped him take a closer look at his own eating habits. He could see her making changes and wanted to take his own situation seriously. Maria describes progress in recovery with a scene from *Harry Potter*. Dumbledore walks down a dark alley lighting lamps one by one. Each lights only a small part of the street. At the far end, he turns and sees what the lights have added up to. That is how daily changes looked to her once she had enough distance to see them together.
